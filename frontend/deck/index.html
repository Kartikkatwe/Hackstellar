A business needs three answers: which problems keep coming back, how many customers each hurts, and which groups are hit hardest. Hard, because complaints come from four channels, the same problem is written a hundred ways in English, Hindi and Hinglish, and one angry customer on three channels is one customer, not three.

Every step is a choice, not writing.

First we find who sent it, so one person on three channels counts once. Is it a complaint? Praise, spam and questions are set aside. Which of seven areas, how serious. Known problem? Yes: filed. Unsure: checked closer. New: waits until three different customers report it, then becomes a named problem. Lost money or a safety risk is urgent from the first report. Every step is a choice from fixed options: that is the idea.

If asked: our side was measured with the real models; the LLM side is projected from calibrated token counts. Total token saving is smaller than the cost saving, because most of our tokens go to a decision model that is far cheaper per token.

The LLM writes only when something new needs a name.

An LLM is a writer: you pay for text and wait for text. Filing a complaint is not writing; it is choices from options we already have. Jev, a decision model, picks an option and returns probabilities, and writes no output tokens. Sure: filed. Unsure: it says so and we pass it on. Cheapest step first: a free local match, Jev's yes or no, and only the last few reach the LLM, whose real job is naming new problems and merging duplicates.

The gate is one call to Jev with six questions. Every answer is a choice with a probability, so an unsure answer is passed on instead of guessed, and nothing is written, so it is fast and cheap. The questions come from the business's settings file: a new business needs no training.

Accuracy

Checked against an answer key.

What is checked

  • Filed under the right issue
  • Real complaints kept
  • Praise and spam removed
  • Sent to the right area
  • Customer links correct

Against what

  • Each complaint's true issue
  • No real complaint dropped
  • None reach the issue list
  • Each complaint's true area
  • Each link's true customer

The engine never sees the answers. Test data is template-made, so real complaints will be messier.

Every test complaint carries a hidden answer: its issue, area and customer. The engine never sees it; we only score with it. Honest: the data was generated from templates and the cut-offs were tuned on it. On real data we would tune on one labelled sample and measure on another.
