Our idea

Choose, don’t write.

LLM a writer

a failed payment where the user…”
Writes for every complaintslow · costly

Jev a decision model

Payments89%
Order accuracy3%
Delivery2%
Refunds2%
Picks from options, writes nothingfast · cheap

The LLM writes only when something new needs a name.

An LLM is a writer: you pay for text and wait for text. Filing a complaint is not writing; it is choices from options we already have. Jev, a decision model, picks an option and returns probabilities, and writes no output tokens. Sure: filed. Unsure: it says so and we pass it on. Cheapest step first: a free local match, Jev's yes or no, and only the last few reach the LLM, whose real job is naming new problems and merging duplicates.

Not instead of Jira: each ranked problem can become one Jira ticket instead of 300 (next step).

Most support teams today run Jira Service Management. It is a good tracker, but its unit is a ticket: one per message. Its AI suggests similar tickets, yet an agent still links them to a problem record, decides the priority, and notices new patterns. One customer writing on email, chat and Twitter is usually three tickets. We do the part before the ticket: every complaint is filed under its recurring problem on arrival, one customer counts once, a new problem gets a name once three customers report it, and the Top 10 comes with a score you can check. We are not asking anyone to drop Jira: the ranked problems can go into it as one ticket each instead of hundreds. That connector is a next step, not built yet.
