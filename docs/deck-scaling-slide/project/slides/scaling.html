Sources: GAEE report citing RedSeer; PIB, 14 Mar 2025; Storyboard18 citing the Consumer Affairs Ministry. Throughput and servers projected from measured latencies; 1–3/sec assumes 1–3% of orders lead to a complaint.

So, first, the scale. Quick commerce in India does around 7.8 million orders a day. Even what reaches the government is big: the consumer helpline takes about five thousand calls a day, and in one year Swiggy alone had about 9,500 complaints escalated to the government, Zomato about 7,000. Those are only the people who gave up on the company. The complaints companies handle themselves are far bigger: by our estimate 80,000 to 2 lakh a day in quick commerce. That sounds like a lot, but it’s only one to three a second on average. Classifying doesn’t share any data, so we just add workers; one does about fifteen a second. Filing changes the issue list, so each business area gets its own single writer, and areas run side by side. New customer? New partitions. Busy area? Split it. Model cost stays around $433 a month at ten million complaints, and a business fits on a server or two. It’s not built at that scale yet, so the first thing we do is load-test it.
